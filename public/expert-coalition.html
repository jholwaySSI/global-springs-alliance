Sanot Adhikari

Founder & Executive Director, Youth Alliance for Environment (YAE)

Region: Nepal · Himalaya · South Asia · Global

Expertise: Mountain Springs · Himalayan Freshwater Systems · Climate Resilience · Environmental Governance

Sanot Adhikari is a Nepalese environmentalist and climate policy expert and the Founder and Executive Director of the Youth Alliance for Environment (YAE). He holds a Master’s degree in Environmental Science from Tribhuvan University, Nepal. With nearly two decades of experience in environmental management, climate change, and sustainable development, his work focuses on Himalayan freshwater systems, mountain springs, climate resilience, and environmental governance.

As a Principal Investigator and Lead Researcher, he has led and contributed to research across mountain watersheds of Nepal, including spring inventories, assessments of water insecurity, and analyses of pollution pressures and the drivers of spring depletion. He has authored and co-authored more than a dozen peer-reviewed publications on spring dynamics, climate vulnerability, freshwater systems, and adaptation.

Adhikari also works at the science–policy–practice interface, collaborating with government institutions, policymakers, parliamentary committees, civil society, and international organizations. His policy contributions include supporting Nepal’s Climate Change Policy, Nationally Determined Contributions (NDC 3.0), Local Adaptation Plans for Action (LAPA), and environmental protection legislation, and he closely follows the UNFCCC process. His work seeks to strengthen spring and freshwater conservation, climate-resilient development, and sustainable environmental governance across the Himalayan region.

Nélida Barajas Acosta

Executive Director, CEDO Intercultural

Region: Mexico · Sonoran Desert · Gulf of California

Expertise: Conservation Leadership · Desert & Marine Ecology · Environmental Education · Community Conservation

Email: nelida@cedo.org

Nélida Barajas Acosta is internationally recognized for her work in desert and marine biology and for her leadership in conservation. As Executive Director of CEDO Intercultural, the Intercultural Center for the Study of Deserts and Oceans, she leads a team dedicated to environmental conservation and education in the Puerto Peñasco region of Mexico.

Founded in the early 1980s, CEDO Intercultural works to promote awareness and understanding of the ecosystems of the Upper Gulf of California and the Sonoran Desert through research, education, and conservation. The organization collaborates with local communities, businesses, and government institutions to advance sustainable practices and environmental awareness. Nélida also serves on the Board of Directors of the International Sonoran Desert Alliance.

Topiltzin Contreras-MacBeath, Ph.D.

Professor of Ichthyology and Conservation Biology, Autonomous University of the State of Morelos

Region: Mexico · Mesoamerica · Global

Expertise: Freshwater Ecosystems · Endangered Fish Conservation · Conservation Biology · Protected Areas

Email: topis@uaem.mx

Dr. Topiltzin “Topis” Contreras-MacBeath has been a professor of ichthyology and conservation biology at the Autonomous University of the State of Morelos in central Mexico for 40 years, where he leads the Conservation Biology work group and has helped train generations of biologists. His research focuses on freshwater ecosystems and the conservation of endangered fishes. Among his most notable achievements is the conservation of the Morelos minnow, Notropis boucardi, a species that was critically endangered and is now protected and valued by local communities.

He served for 23 years as President of the advisory committee of the Corredor Biológico Chichinautzin Natural Protected Area, working with researchers, local authorities, and stakeholders on sustainable management strategies. He is a founder and Regional Coordinator for Mesoamerica of the IUCN SSC Freshwater Fish Specialist Group, a founder and Co-Chair of the IUCN SSC Freshwater Conservation Committee, and a member of the IUCN Species Survival Commission Steering Committee. He previously served as Minister of Sustainable Development for the Government of the State of Morelos. An accomplished photographer, he also helped establish the Alianza Mexicana de Fotografía para la Conservación in 2020 to advance conservation through photography.

Michael Edmondstone

Communications and Engagement Lead, SHOAL

Region: Global

Expertise: Freshwater Conservation Communications · Public Engagement · Biodiversity Awareness

Email: m.edmondstone@shoal.org

Michael Edmondstone is the Communications and Engagement Lead at SHOAL, where he works to raise awareness of the freshwater biodiversity crisis and the conservation efforts underway to address it, while inspiring people about the extraordinary diversity of life in the planet’s freshwaters.

Michael has worked across a range of communications roles, including as a journalist in Tanzania and as a marketing manager at a media agency. After spending much of a decade in the corporate sector, he joined an environmental NGO as a communications officer, where he felt he could make a more positive contribution to the challenges facing the natural world. He joined SHOAL in 2020 and enjoys the challenge of drawing attention to freshwater species that have historically been overlooked.

Dean Gilligan, Ph.D.

Freshwater & Wetlands Ecologist, Bush Heritage Australia

Region: Australia · Oceania · Global

Expertise: Springs Ecology · Freshwater Fish Conservation · Conservation Genetics · Threatened Species Recovery

Dr. Dean Gilligan is a freshwater ecologist and conservation biologist with 25 years of professional experience studying Australia’s freshwater fishes and the rivers, lakes, wetlands, and springs they inhabit. He holds a Ph.D. in conservation genetics from Macquarie University and serves as Freshwater & Wetlands Ecologist with Bush Heritage Australia, where he works to protect the Great Artesian Basin spring ecosystems at Edgbaston Special Wildlife Reserve in central-west Queensland.

His work spans long-term environmental health monitoring, threatened species recovery, invasive species management, and freshwater biodiversity conservation. He currently chairs the national Red-finned Blue-eye recovery team. As a 2024 Churchill Fellow, he investigated international best practices for conserving freshwater spring ecosystems in Mexico and the United States to help strengthen conservation of Australia’s unique Great Artesian Basin springs.

Ian Harrison, Ph.D.

Co-chair, IUCN SSC Freshwater Conservation Committee · Adjunct Professor, Northern Arizona University

Region: Global

Expertise: Freshwater Fish Conservation · Systematic Ichthyology · Freshwater Biodiversity

Email: harrisonffsg@gmail.com

Dr. Ian Harrison obtained his Ph.D. in systematic ichthyology at the University of Bristol, UK. He has conducted research on marine and freshwater fishes from several parts of the world, including fieldwork in Europe, Central and South America, West and Western Central Africa, the Philippines, and the Central Pacific. He was based at the American Museum of Natural History from 1996 to 2008, conducting research on systematic ichthyology and freshwater conservation biology, before working with Conservation International (CI) until 2024. He has served as the Technical Officer for the Freshwater Fish Specialist Group of IUCN’s Species Survival Commission (SSC) and is co-Chair of the IUCN SSC Freshwater Conservation Committee. He is also co-Chair of the SSC/WCPA Task Force on Dams, a member of the Mohamed bin Zayed Species Conservation Fund’s Grant Advisory Board, and a member of the Science Committee of the National Geographic Society and Chubb Charitable Foundation Blue Boundaries Program. He is an Adjunct Professor for the School of Earth and Sustainability at Northern Arizona University. He is currently based in Flagstaff, Arizona.

Joseph Holway, Ph.D.

Incoming Director, Springs Stewardship Institute · Co-Chair, IUCN Global Springs Task Force

Region: North America · Global

Expertise: Springs Ecology · Ecohydrology · Conservation Science · Data Synthesis

Email: joseph@springstewardship.org

Dr. Joseph Holway is a conservation scientist whose work integrates springs ecology, hydrology, field science, and large-scale data analysis. He first joined the Springs Stewardship Institute as a field technician in 2015 and has since returned to help lead efforts to inventory, monitor, and conserve spring ecosystems across the American Southwest and beyond. He earned his Ph.D. in Environmental Life Sciences from Arizona State University in 2024, where his research examined hydrologic variability and food security in the Lower Mekong Basin, and he was a Fulbright Research Scholar in Cambodia in 2021. His current work focuses on expanding the scientific understanding of springs, strengthening global data and monitoring systems, and translating ecological information into practical conservation action. He also serves as Co-Chair of the IUCN Global Springs Task Force.

Alejandro Varela-Romero, Ph.D.

Professor and Full-time Researcher, University of Sonora

Region: Mexico · Sonoran Desert · Northwestern Mexico

Expertise: Freshwater Fish Conservation · Conservation Genetics · Ichthyology · Systematics

Email: alejandro.varela@unison.mx

Dr. Alejandro Varela-Romero is a professor and full-time researcher in the Department of Scientific and Technological Research at the University of Sonora, where he works in conservation biology and ecology. His research focuses on aquatic-resource conservation, with particular emphasis on conservation genetics, freshwater fish ecology, ichthyology, systematics, and endangered species in northwestern Mexico. He is responsible for the Conservation Genetics Laboratory and the Native Fish Collection at DICTUS and has led or contributed to long-running work on native fishes of the Sonoran region, including Yaqui catfish and Sonoyta pupfish.

His research record spans genetics, taxonomy, ecology, and conservation of freshwater and marine fishes, including recent work using environmental DNA to assess occupancy of the endangered Yaqui catfish. He earned a doctorate in sciences from the Centro de Investigación en Alimentación y Desarrollo in 2007 and is recognized in Mexico’s National System of Researchers at Level II.

Zeb Hogan, Ph.D.

Research Professor, University of Nevada, Reno · Director, Wonders of the Mekong

Region: Global · North America · Southeast Asia

Expertise: Freshwater Fish Conservation · Fish Migration · Endangered Species · Conservation Biology

Email: zhogan@unr.edu

Dr. Zeb Hogan is a research professor in the Department of Biology at the University of Nevada, Reno and Director of the Wonders of the Mekong project. For more than two decades, his work has focused on the ecology and conservation of freshwater fishes, especially large-bodied, migratory, and threatened species, while connecting conservation science with public education and action.

His research has contributed to understanding fish migration, population status, and conservation threats in freshwater systems around the world, including the Mekong Basin. He has helped raise international awareness of endangered freshwater fishes and their habitats and hosts the National Geographic television series Monster Fish with Zeb Hogan.
